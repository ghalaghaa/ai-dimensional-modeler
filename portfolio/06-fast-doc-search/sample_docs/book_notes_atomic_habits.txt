Book Notes: Atomic Habits by James Clear

Core idea: small, consistent habits compound into remarkable results over
time. A 1% improvement each day, sustained for a year, leads to dramatic
change because the gains build on each other.

Key concepts:
1. The Four Laws of Behavior Change
   - Make it obvious (cue)
   - Make it attractive (craving)
   - Make it easy (response)
   - Make it satisfying (reward)

2. Identity-based habits: focus on who you want to become rather than
   what you want to achieve. Every action is a vote for the type of
   person you wish to be. Instead of "I want to read more," think
   "I am someone who reads every day."

3. Habit stacking: attach a new habit to an existing one using the
   formula "After [current habit], I will [new habit]." This makes new
   routines easier to remember and start.

4. Environment design matters more than willpower. Make good habits
   obvious and easy by shaping your surroundings, and make bad habits
   invisible and hard by adding friction.

5. The two-minute rule: when starting a new habit, it should take less
   than two minutes to do. "Read before bed" becomes "read one page."

Favorite quote: "You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to
the level of your systems."

Personal takeaway: I want to apply habit stacking to build a daily
reading habit and use environment design to reduce phone usage before
bed.
